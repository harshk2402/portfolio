Derivatives Pricing Engine in C++: Binomial Trees, Black–Scholes & Monte Carlo

C++
Numerical Methods
Monte Carlo
Repository

A dependency-free C++ library that prices options three independent ways: binomial trees, closed-form Black–Scholes and Monte Carlo simulation. Because the methods overlap, each one checks the others. It covers European and American vanilla options, digital and spread payoffs, path-dependent Asian options with control variates, multi-asset basket options, implied volatility and bond yields. Models, contracts and numerical tools are kept separate, so adding a new payoff means writing one small subclass, and every pricer then handles it.

  • Modules: binomial, analytic, montecarlo (with a multi-asset basket sub-module), fixedincome and numerics. Each has its own namespace, and together they build as one CMake library.
  • Models vs. contracts: a model (binomial tree, geometric Brownian motion) describes how the underlying moves. A contract only knows its payoff, through a virtual Payoff; pricers combine the two.
  • One solver, many problems: bisection and Newton–Raphson work on a generic Function interface. The same code finds a bond's yield and an option's implied volatility.
  • Composable Monte Carlo: the price, standard error and Greeks are stored on the option, so a control variate can combine one simulation with another option's closed-form price.

  • Binomial trees: the Cox–Ross–Rubinstein model with an arbitrage check. European prices come from both the closed-form CRR formula and backward induction. The library also gives the replicating portfolio (stock and cash positions) at every node, and prices American options by the Snell envelope, including where exercise is optimal.
  • Payoffs: calls, puts, digitals, bull and bear spreads, strangles, butterflies and double digitals.
  • Closed form: Black–Scholes price, delta and vega; generalized Black–Scholes with a cost-of-carry term, covering dividend-paying stocks, futures and currencies; implied volatility.
  • Monte Carlo: exact simulation of geometric Brownian motion paths, with standard errors. Delta and gamma come from rescaling the simulated paths, so the bumped prices reuse the same random numbers. Arithmetic Asian calls are priced with the geometric Asian call as a closed-form control variate.
  • Multi-asset: correlated assets driven by a volatility matrix, basket Asian calls, and a delta for each asset computed on common random numbers.

  • Cross-checks: the CRR formula and backward induction agree to machine precision, and the replicating portfolio costs exactly the option price. The American call matches the European call, as it should with no dividends. Monte Carlo agrees with the closed form to within one standard error.
  • Variance reduction: on a one-month arithmetic Asian call, the geometric control variate cuts the standard error from about 0.012 to 0.00014, roughly 85× smaller.
  • Testing: 97 regression checks run in CI on Ubuntu and macOS. Deterministic results are checked to a relative tolerance of 10−11 and Monte Carlo results statistically, and every example program also runs as a smoke test.

Key Features

  • Binomial pricing of European, American and exotic payoffs, with replicating portfolios
  • Closed-form Black–Scholes, generalized for dividends, futures and FX, plus implied volatility
  • Monte Carlo for Asian options, with control variates and pathwise Greeks
  • Correlated multi-asset basket options with a delta for each asset
  • Generic root finding and quadrature reused across pricing problems
  • 97 regression tests, an example for every method, and CI on Linux and macOS
